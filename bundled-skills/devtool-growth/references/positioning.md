# Positioning

## The core rule: lead with the PROBLEM, ride the rising TERM, never lead with the category label

Developer markets punish hype and category-label openers. Three layers:

1. **Lead with the concrete problem/outcome**, not the category. "I got tired of X, so I built Y" beats "Y is a [category]." On 2026 HN/Reddit/X-dev, "another agent harness / wrapper / framework / chatbot / workflow builder" is an instant fatigue-bury regardless of quality.
2. **Find the rising category TERM the thought-leaders are pushing and own it as a *product*.** When O'Reilly / a big DevRel voice / a major lab is busy legitimizing a term (e.g. "context engineering" in '24-'25, "loop engineering" in '26), there is search demand + air cover and *almost nobody has claimed it as a product yet*. Be the noun for the verb everyone's discussing. Lots of think-pieces + no product = your lane.
3. **Win with proof, not the buzzword.** The term is the billboard; concrete mechanics are the close. A vaporware tool slapping on the trendy term gets called out instantly; you won't, *if you actually shipped the substance*. That asymmetry is the whole game. Only ride a term you genuinely earn.

## The ladder framing (reusable)
Position your tool as the top rung of a progression the audience already believes in. Example that works for AI dev tools:
- Prompt engineering → controls one turn.
- Context engineering → controls what the model sees.
- **Loop engineering → controls the iteration** (plan → act → verify → keep what passed → repeat, without drift).

A progression makes your category feel *inevitable and next*, not *crowded*.

## Traps to refuse (name the bias out loud)

- **Founder-excitement bias → hype register.** Wanting your tool to "DOMINATE / be the MASTER BLASTER" is fine energy, wrong expression for devs. Consumer markets reward loud; **developer-infrastructure markets punish it**, every prior data layer showed hype gets buried/ratio'd and concrete+honest converts. The dev translation of "dominance" = *become the obvious default and own the category word*, earned via the demo and credibility, not slogans.
- **The "company of agents / AI software org" trap.** Multi-agent "org chart" framing is heavily saturated (MetaGPT ~50k★ owns "build a software company from a prompt"; CrewAI/AutoGen too). A fleet of role-agents is a great *demo/wow*, a weak *hero*, skeptics attack it first ("prove a fake org writes better code"). Keep it as the test drive, not the billboard.
- **Category-lie positioning.** Don't claim a capability the tool doesn't have (e.g. branding a dev tool as a "sales machine"). Devs detect it in seconds and it destroys trust.

## Honesty as a differentiator
For dev tools, *stating the limits* ("the denylist isn't a complete control, that's why the OS sandbox is the real boundary") builds more trust than polish. Lead skeptical audiences with candor.

## Secondary ROI/business track (optional, different buyer)
If a business-value angle is wanted, target **engineering managers**, not devs: "ship faster, safer, cheaper, verified runs + a cost ledger." That's a legitimate ROI pitch to a different buyer, distinct from the dev-facing problem-led pitch. Don't conflate the two in one piece of copy.
